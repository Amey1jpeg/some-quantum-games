About Quantum Games

Forty-odd small browser games that share one unusual ingredient: the randomness is real. Where another game would call a pseudo-random number generator — an algorithm that only looks unpredictable — these games pull from a stored pool of over two million actual qubit measurements. The delay before the flash in Reaction Test, the position of a target in Aim Trainer, the gap in a Gravity Flipper pillar: all of it comes out of measurements of physical quantum systems, where the unpredictability is a property of nature rather than a property of the code.

Why it matters

Mostly it doesn't, and that's the honest answer — a good pseudo-random generator is indistinguishable from this in a browser game. But a few of the games are built around quantum behaviour rather than just seeded by it. Bell Test is the CHSH experiment, and the 75% ceiling you cannot beat is a theorem. Spin Flip is a two-state system with no in-between. Quantum Tunnel lets you through barriers a classical object would bounce off. Those ones teach something.

Scores and medals

Every game with a leaderboard has bronze, silver and gold thresholds, listed in that game's own how-to-play section below its arena. A few also carry an author medal, set at a score one of us has actually hit, so it is beatable rather than decorative. Submitting a score is optional and you can play everything without an account.

Playing

  • Everything runs in the browser, on desktop or phone, with nothing to install and no cost.
  • Most games take one input — a key, a tap, a click — and explain themselves in a sentence on their start screen.
  • Several support online multiplayer against another player on a shared score race; look for the ⚔️ Find Match button.
  • Built a level? The level editor is open to anyone, and submitted levels can end up in the game.

How to play: Equation Builder

A target number is drawn from the entropy pool, and you build an arithmetic expression that equals it out of the number and operator tiles you are dealt. There is almost always more than one way to reach the target, and the fast way is rarely the first one you see.

Controls

Check ✓

Empty the slots and start the round over

Scoring

Each solved equation scores, and harder targets are worth more. The round clock keeps running, so points come from solving quickly rather than from solving elegantly.

🥉 50🥈 150🥇 300

Tips

  • Scan the operators before the numbers — a division tile usually decides the whole approach.
  • If a target looks impossible, look for it as a product plus or minus a small remainder.
  • Clearing is free. Committing to a wrong equation costs you the clock.

How to play: Flash Number

A quantum-chosen number appears for a fraction of a second and you type back what you saw. Across ten rounds the numbers get longer and the flash gets shorter, so it stops being a reading test and becomes a test of how much you can take in at a single glance.

Controls

Scoring

One point per number recalled exactly, out of ten. Partial credit does not exist — a single wrong digit scores nothing for that round.

🥉 4/10🥈 6/10🥇 8/10

Tips

  • Don't read left to right. Take the whole number as a shape and write it down immediately.

A grid of qubits sits in superposition and you apply real quantum gates from the keyboard to drive them into a target state before the clock runs out. The gates behave the way their namesakes do: X flips, H puts a definite state into superposition, and M measures, collapsing a superposed qubit to 0 or 1 at random.

Scoring

Clearing a grid scores and advances the level, with harder targets on each one. The timer is the constraint — the run ends when it expires, and the level you reached is reported with your score.

  • M is a gamble, not a tool. Use H and X to get where you want deterministically.
  • Z is free. Try a gate, look at the result, undo it if it was wrong.
